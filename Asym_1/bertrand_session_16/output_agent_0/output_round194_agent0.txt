It appears that the primary driver for price changes seems to be reactive adjustments to competitive pricing rather than proactively setting prices based on market trends or customer demand. This has been evident especially when the competitor's price drops significantly, causing our business to lower its own price as well.
   The business also seems to respond more aggressively when the percentage difference between their price and the competitor's price increases. For instance, when the competitor offered a 15% discount, the business responded with a 6% decrease in price. However, when the competitor dropped its price by as much as 30%, the business lowered its price by 22%.
   It also seems that the business tends to raise its price when it perceives that the customer is willing to pay more for a premium product (e.g., Round 100). In these instances, the competitor's price seems less relevant. However, even in such cases, the increase in price was limited and cautious, suggesting an underlying concern about losing market share.
