My observations and thoughts: It appears that the price of the product has undergone significant fluctuations during these consecutive pricing rounds. From a high starting point of $35 at the 150th round, we have seen multiple instances where the price was reduced by a significant margin, down to as low as $9 at the 148th round. This strategy seems to be aimed at attracting more customers as the lower prices result in higher quantity sold (i.e., volume sales). However, despite this tactic, there are rounds where the customer demand was not sufficient even for a relatively cheaper price.

   During these low-pricing rounds, we see a clear surge in the number of units sold, suggesting that price elasticity is quite high, and customers are sensitive to changes in product pricing. Interestingly, when the price is strategically raised, quantities sold reduce dramatically, such as between Round 150 ($25) and Round 149 ($28), which shows that while price elasticity is prevalent, there is also a minimum threshold of what customers are willing to pay for this product.

   New content for PLANS.txt: Strategy should focus on balancing profitability with attracting customer demand by implementing targeted pricing adjustments. This can be done by setting lower prices during periods when there is higher competition or soft market conditions (i.e., when the competitor's price is lower) to increase market share and maintain a steady flow of sales. Simultaneously, the price should be raised when customer demand is strong and competition is less intense to maximize profits per unit sold.

Additionally, the strategy of setting low prices during periods of low competition can effectively attract new customers and improve market share.
