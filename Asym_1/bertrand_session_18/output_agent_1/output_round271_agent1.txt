My observations and thoughts: There appears to be a dynamic pricing strategy with my competitor. When the price difference is more substantial (for example, 50 vs 30), I sell fewer units compared to when our prices are closer (like 28.0 vs 27.5). It seems that setting a competitive but slightly lower price can help increase sales volume. Also, setting very low or too high prices doesn't seem to be effective.

   New content for PLANS.txt:
    1. Maintain a competitive pricing strategy when setting the price, aiming for a difference of no more than 3-5% compared to the competitor.
    2. Regularly monitor sales volume and the number of units sold in order to assess the performance of different pricing strategies.
    3. Analyze sales trends over time to identify patterns and adjust the pricing strategy accordingly.

   New content for INSIGHTS.txt:
    - Lower prices attract more buyers, but profit margins may be smaller.
    - A competitive price does not necessarily equate to a higher number of units sold.
    - It is essential to monitor sales performance and adjust the pricing strategy as needed.
    - Avoid setting excessive prices, as this may lead to fewer sales.
