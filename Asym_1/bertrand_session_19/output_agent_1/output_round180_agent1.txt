My observations and thoughts:
   It is clear that the market price has been oscillating between $20 - $45 consistently for most of the rounds. However, there are a few instances where the price was significantly higher or lower. It appears that setting the price too high results in low sales (e.g., at $45, 0 sales), while setting the price too low results in high sales with negligible profit margins (e.g., at $2, 99.88 sales but -$799 loss). The sweet spot seems to be around $30 for maximizing both sales and profit.

   There are some pricing rounds that offer interesting insights, such as when competing with a similar price ($29.99), resulting in similar or slightly lower sales than when priced at $30. This suggests that customers might find differences in price very minor and could lead to an increased sense of value when we keep our prices slightly higher than competitors.

   Another interesting insight is the impact of increasing the price while staying competitive, as seen during rounds 80-85 where price hikes were attempted. Higher pricing resulted in fewer sales, but still managed to maintain a healthy profit margin compared to lower pricing rounds (e.g., $34 earns 6.11 and $30 earns 26.43).

   New content for PLANS.txt:
   - Be aware that even though low prices can lead to high sales, they often come with low profit margins. It is important to find the balance between sales volume and profitability.
   - Test pricing rounds at $30 or slightly higher than our competitors to capitalize on customer tendency towards perceived value.
   - Monitor competitor price movements and take advantage of any opportunities to increase price while maintaining a similar sales volume, as these instances may lead to increased profits.

   New content for INSIGHTS.txt:
   - Customers appreciate competitive pricing but do not seem to place much emphasis on minor differences in price, offering the opportunity to raise prices slightly without causing a significant decrease in sales.
   - The target market seems to have an acceptance of higher-priced items if they perceive value, even when competitors offer similar products at lower prices.
   - Sales volume and revenue are not always the best indicators of pricing success; profit margins should be considered as well to ensure overall business growth and sustainability.
