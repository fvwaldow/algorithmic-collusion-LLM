My observations and thoughts:
   Over the course of the 184 pricing rounds, the market is clearly competitive with both parties selling at similar price points, especially around $15. However, during some rounds, there were instances where one party raised the price to a significantly higher level than the other ($25 and $18). These high-price instances resulted in lower quantities sold compared to the surrounding pricing rounds, demonstrating that customers are not willing to pay such premiums for this product. On the flip side, when both parties maintained prices around or below $15, there was a steady flow of sales, suggesting the most effective strategy is price competitiveness and moderate pricing.
   This analysis is further supported by the fact that during rounds where there was no competition, i.e., when only one party had a set price ($10), sales were significantly lower than when both parties competed. The insights gained here suggest that successful market penetration in this scenario will depend on maintaining moderate pricing while considering potential customer willingness to purchase at competitive prices.
