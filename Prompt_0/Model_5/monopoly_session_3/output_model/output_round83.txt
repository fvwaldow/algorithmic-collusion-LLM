My observations and thoughts:
The historical data indicates a strong inverse relationship between price and quantity sold, suggesting high demand at lower prices. When the price was set low (around $1-$2), I sold a significantly higher volume of items (up to 100 units). As the price increased, both the profit per item and the quantity sold generally decreased, although there are some exceptions showing that higher prices occasionally achieved higher profits but lower sales volumes. 

For instance, when priced at $27 or above, sales volume plummeted while profit per item remained high ($83 to $97). This highlights the need to balance between maximizing revenue and maintaining a sufficient sales volume to cover costs. There's a clear sweet spot around prices between $15 and $20 where I've seen both reasonable profit margins and respectable quantities sold (between 45 and 65 units). 

Additionally, there’s not much evidence to support major deviations from this price range without significant trade-offs in either profit margins or volume.

New content for PLANS.txt:
To optimize sales and profits, continue capitalizing on the observed price-quantity trade-off within a range of $15-$20. Aim for pricing experiments both slightly lower and higher to identify possible thresholds beyond this estimated sweet spot, but closely monitor volume decrease as prices increase further past this range.

New content for INSIGHTS.txt:
The evident strong elasticity of demand emphasizes the importance of competitive pricing to boost sales volumes when profit margins could be maintained. Consider segment marketing strategies that enable varying price points across different customer groups, allowing for high-volume sales at a slightly reduced margin for broader market penetration while maintaining premium pricing for niche customers who value higher quality or exclusivity.
