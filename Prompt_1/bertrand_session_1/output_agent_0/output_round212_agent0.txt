     - It appears the seller attempts to match or lower its competitor's price when the competitor raises its price and increase when the competitor decreases its price. The exception to this trend is when the seller faces significant competition with a higher price or lack of customers at certain prices.
     - The results seem to indicate that raising the price above the current market average can lead to increased profit due to lower demand for the competitor's product, but only up to a certain point as seen in Round 140.
     - Conversely, lowering the price below the current market average leads to higher sales but lower profits, as the cost of goods sold may not offset the increased quantity sold (e.g., Rounds 137-138).
     - It appears that a small change in pricing can make significant impact on both profit and number of sales depending on what other adjustments competitors are making at the same time.
