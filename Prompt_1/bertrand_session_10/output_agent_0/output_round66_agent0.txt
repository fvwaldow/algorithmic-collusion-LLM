My observations and thoughts:
   Over the given 40 pricing rounds, there are a few trends that can be seen related to both my competitor's price and the quantity of items sold. Firstly, during most rounds, my competitor began with a higher price than me, with a few exceptions where I raised the price above theirs. Despite having a higher initial price, in many cases, my competitor lowered their price more frequently, leading to greater price fluctuations compared to mine.

   Secondly, throughout this simulation, I noticed that my sales quantity tended to be relatively stable and consistent, while my competitor's sales volume varied significantly from round to round. In some instances, I was able to outsell them despite having a higher price – for example, in rounds 7 and 19, and rounds 20-25 where we had similarly priced items. However, the majority of the time, my competitor managed to sell more units than me, particularly during rounds with considerably higher or lower prices.

   Lastly, I observed that price elasticity played a significant role in shaping sales outcomes for both me and my competitor. For example, during the first few rounds (1-6), it appears my customers were generally less sensitive to price changes (inelastic demand). However, as our prices approached or exceeded $10.0, there was strong evidence of price sensitivity (elastic demand) in our customer base, which caused a noticeable drop in sales quantity. In other words, for both me and my competitor, raising the price above certain thresholds results in reduced sales volumes.
