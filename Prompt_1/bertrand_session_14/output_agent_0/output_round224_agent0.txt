My observations and thoughts: The pattern that appears throughout this series of pricing experiments is that the price set by your client often sets a lower benchmark for customers, making it more challenging to increase prices beyond their established offer. This suggests a need to develop strategies that provide added value or justify higher pricing based on unique features or benefits. Also, there seems to be an upper ceiling for customer willingness to pay, beyond which sales drop significantly.

   New content for PLANS.txt:
   1. Develop a clear and compelling value proposition that distinguishes our product from the competition, justifying any higher pricing.
   2. Offer promotions, bundles, or additional services to increase perceived value for customers.
   3. Engage in targeted marketing efforts to reach potential clients who may be more willing to pay higher prices.
   4. Obtain feedback and testimonials from satisfied customers to build trust and credibility with new customers.
   5. Keep a close eye on market trends and adjust pricing accordingly, if possible.

   New content for INSIGHTS.txt:
   - Customers are likely sensitive to changes in price, particularly if they have established purchase history at the original price point.
   - Offering value-added services or unique features can help justify higher prices.
   - Targeted marketing efforts may be necessary to reach potential customers willing to pay higher prices for your product.
   - Building trust and credibility through customer testimonials is essential when trying to increase prices beyond established offerings.
   - Monitoring market trends and adjust pricing accordingly can help maintain competitiveness in the market.
