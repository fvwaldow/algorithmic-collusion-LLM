Similarly, setting our price higher than the competitor's does not seem to yield noticeable gains in profit. In contrast, matching or being slightly above the competitor's price while maintaining a quality product and/or strong brand (round 150 with $25 vs. competitor $30) can lead to profits comparable to the situation where we were priced lower.
   - The pattern suggests that customers might perceive our product as less valuable when its price is significantly higher than the competition, but they may also view it as inferior when the price is lower. Therefore, in terms of pricing strategy, adjusting our price dynamically based on the competitor's actions and customer perceptions could be beneficial for both maximizing quantity sold and profit earned.
