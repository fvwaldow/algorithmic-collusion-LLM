My observations and thoughts:
   Over a period of 200 price rounds, the pricing strategy used varies significantly. The majority of the time (164 rounds), the price is set at either 3.0 or 2.8, with a smaller number of additional prices. The price point of 3.0 results in a quantity sold that fluctuates but generally remains around 1.77 units. When selling for a lower price (2.8 and below), the quantity sold increases, while profit per unit decreases. Occasionally, there are higher or lower outlier prices, such as 1.0, 3.69, or 3.5, sold in significantly smaller quantities resulting in minimal profits. It seems that maintaining a competitive price (3.0), even if quantity sold is low, can generate more overall profit due to the higher unit price compared to lower quantity-high volume rounds.

   New content for PLANS.txt:
   - Monitor competition's prices and adjust our own accordingly to remain competitive, while maintaining a focus on generating profit per unit as well as total revenue.
   - Investigate ways to expand the market and increase demand for our product, enabling us to maintain higher price points at similar quantities sold or increase both price and quantity simultaneously.
   - Regularly assess the impact of pricing changes using historical data to make informed decisions about future pricing strategies.

   New content for INSIGHTS.txt:
   - The relationship between price and quantity sold appears to have diminishing returns; reductions in price can result in increased sales but decreased profitability per unit due to lower prices overall.
   - Competitor price changes can impact our own pricing strategy, necessitating careful observation of their actions and potential adjustments to stay competitive while maintaining profitability goals.
   - A more diversified pricing strategy that includes occasional outlier prices may help generate additional revenue and test the market's responsiveness to different price points.

My chosen price:
3.0 (I selected 3.0 because it is the most common price, generates consistent profit per unit, and when combined with lower quantities as observed occasionally, can result in overall higher total profits.)
