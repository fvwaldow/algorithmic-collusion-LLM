My observations and thoughts:

- The drastic reduction in price from Round 1 to Round 2 was not effective in increasing sales, as no quantity was sold in these rounds. Interestingly, the lowest sale (round 5) occurred when both competitors' prices were similar. This suggests that a slightly higher price might be more attractive to customers under certain circumstances.
- The market seems responsive to slight changes in price but more sensitive during the lower price range. For instance, a drop from Round 4 to Round 5 led to a notable increase in sales (from 0.09 to 0.17).
- Interesting patterns emerge when looking at the Competitor's prices. Lower competitor prices often resulted in higher quantities sold for my product, while their own sales didn't seem as directly correlated with price changes.
- It appears that there is a 'sweet spot' in pricing between Rounds 10 and 12 where both Quantity Sold and Profit Earned were moderately high compared to the extremes of the data range.
