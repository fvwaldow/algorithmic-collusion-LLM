My observations and thoughts: Our business has gone through some interesting price changes over the last few rounds. Initially, we started with a higher price point of 14.99, which did not result in any sales as our competitor was offering their product at a lower price of 7.5. We then lowered our price to match our competitor at 7.49 without success. It seemed that customers preferred our competitor's offering.

   Next, we tried raising our competitor's price to 10.99, which slightly increased our sales volume. However, a sharp decrease in price to 6.98 and 6.79 resulted in an uptick in sales for our competitor but not for us at these lower prices.

   Interestingly, a slight increase in our price to 7.5 did generate some sales, though not as much as when competitors raised their prices. In general, it seems that customers are sensitive to price changes and appreciate slightly lower prices compared to direct competition but are still more likely to choose the cheaper option if the difference is significant.

   PLANS.txt:
   - Monitor and respond accordingly to competitor price movements
   - Continuously evaluate the appropriate pricing strategy while keeping an eye on customer preferences
   - Establish a competitive advantage with value-added features or services wherever possible

   INSIGHTS.txt:
   - Customers are sensitive to price changes, although not always reactive to minor adjustments in pricing
   - Competitor's lower prices have impacted sales volume, but there is still room for our product to be competitive at slightly higher prices than direct competition
   - Customers may be attracted by value-added features or services when comparing options
